How to Solve the 12-Ball Challenge

Example for 12 balls (other paths possible). Always start with: 4 vs 4 balls.

Scenario A: The scale is NOT in balance (4 vs 4)

We now have 4 "potentially heavy", 4 "potentially light", and 4 "guaranteed real" balls.
2nd Weighing: Pan 1 [1 light, 2 heavy] vs. Pan 2 [1 heavy, 2 real].

  • A1) Pan 1 is heavier: Either one of the 2 heavy balls in Pan 1 is the culprit (and is heavy), or the 1 heavy ball in Pan 2 is real and the light one in Pan 1 is actually light. A 3rd weighing of the 2 heavy balls clarifies this.
  • A2) Balance: The culprit is the one "potentially light" ball left out. Weigh it against a "real" ball to see if it's lighter or heavier.
  • A3) Pan 2 is heavier: Either the heavy ball in Pan 2 is the culprit, or the light ball in Pan 1 is light. Weigh one of them against a "real" ball to confirm.

Scenario B: The scale is in Balance (4 vs 4)

We have identified 8 "real" balls. 4 remain untested.
2nd Weighing: Pan 1 [1 real, 2 untested] vs. Pan 2 [2 real, 1 untested].

  • B1) Pan 1 is heavier: Either one of the 2 untested in the heavy pan is heavy, or the 1 untested in the light pan is light. Weigh the 2 "potentially heavy" balls against each other to find out.
  • B2) Balance: The 4th untested ball is the culprit! Weigh it against a "real" one to see its weight.
  • B3) Pan 2 is heavier: Symmetrical to B1. Either the 2 untested in the light pan are light, or the 1 in the heavy pan is heavy.

The Hypothesis Approach (General Solution)

To solve the puzzle for any number of balls, a Boolean Construct is used. For every ball, there are 2 hypotheses: "heavy" or "light".

Each weighing is a mathematical filter. We analyze all possible weighings and their outcomes (Left, Balance, Right). Each weighing is weighted by the minimum number of hypotheses it excludes across all three results. The AI chooses the weighing with the highest weight (Minimax strategy), collects the result, and repeats the process with the remaining set of hypotheses.
